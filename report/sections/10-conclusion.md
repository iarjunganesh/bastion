## 10. Conclusion

Bastion makes an agent fleet's decision path checkable. It restricts what reaches the model, compares the model's structured answer with canonical findings inside the worker, applies policy without a model, and requires confirmations before an investigation completes. Five dated failures between August and November 2026 showed why model-free policy execution, explicit handoffs and completion confirmations are needed. The failures involved a skipped policy step, a report stranded in a worker's session, a decision lost at an A2A hop, a failed remote task delivered as an empty event, and an empty Runtime stream recorded as a completed investigation. False completion was observed in D1, D9 and E19; D6's caller refused the missing report, and D8's terminal status is not independently established. The repairs made policy execution, handoffs and downstream completion explicitly checkable.

The final bounded evaluation covered G and H on v0.9.0 and I–M on v0.9.1, observing the controls within those measured cases. A serial cohort recorded 98 of 98 validated investigations before an instrument stop. The completion, replay, refusal and routing-provenance controls passed. An injected worker timeout failed closed through five attempts and reached dead-letter review. The same evaluation retained two stopped cohorts, an instrument failure and a preflight stop, and it recorded two limitations that the controls do not answer: incomplete Runtime trace export and continued worker computation after a caller-visible timeout.

The supported lesson is narrow and practical. In a distributed agent system, application-level correctness claims require evidence at execution boundaries. The absence of an error, a populated field or a closed stream is not evidence that the work ran.
